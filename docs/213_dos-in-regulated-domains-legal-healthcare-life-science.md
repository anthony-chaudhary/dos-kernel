# 213 — DOS in regulated domains: legal, healthcare, life science

> **The one-line claim.** Regulated domains — law, clinical medicine, financial
> compliance, life science QA — are the domains where an over-claim kills you.
> An agent that confidently narrates "I verified the citation / approved the
> dose / confirmed the filing" in a regulated setting is not a helpful agent; it
> is a liability. DOS's value here is the same as everywhere, stated more sharply:
> **verify the substrate, abstain on the judgment, attach the grade, and refuse
> to let a fluent narration close a loop that requires a non-agent witness.**
> That narrower claim is exactly what these domains' own rules demand — the
> certifying attorney, the QA release, the medical monitor — and it is the only
> honest description of what a trust substrate can do at the Tier-3 ceiling.

This is an **exploration / design note**, not a build plan with measured results.
Companion to docs/212 (the general world-witness map that establishes the
tier taxonomy this doc applies), docs/204 (the four walls — every limit below
is one), and docs/277 (which grounds these same arguments in 2026 frontier
benchmarks). Where I have only reasoning and no measurement, I say so.

---

## 1. Why regulated domains are the sharpest test of the honest ceiling

Every regulated domain has the same architectural feature: a **mandated
non-agent consumer** — a person or process that *must* independently verify the
agent's output before it takes legal or clinical effect. The certifying attorney
signs the brief. The QA release owner signs the batch record. The medical
monitor signs off on the adverse-event report. The compliance officer certifies
the filing.

That mandated consumer is not an accident of bureaucracy. It is the field's
own recognition that the deliverable is too consequential to close on the
agent's (or the author's) say-so. DOS's architecture makes the same bet — every
claim needs a non-forgeable witness — and these domains have already encoded it
in law or regulation.

The consequence for DOS is structural: **in regulated domains the mandated
consumer is a natural non-agent consumer**, the one shape that has consistently
extracted real value from a trust substrate (the EnterpriseOps pattern,
docs/143). The gate does not need to convince anyone that independent
verification is a good idea; the regulation already requires it. DOS just needs
to be the non-forgeable layer that makes that verification cheaper and
auditable.

---

## 2. What DOS abstains on — the Tier-3 ceiling

Before going further, state the ceiling clearly, because in regulated domains
an over-claim is a *liability*, not a bug. See §6.

**DOS abstains on the gestalt** in every domain below. The gestalt is:

- In law: *is the legal argument sound? will the motion succeed?*
- In clinical medicine: *is this the right diagnosis? is this treatment indicated?*
- In financial compliance: *is this the correct regulatory interpretation? is the
  advice sound?*
- In life science QA: *is this batch safe and effective?*

These are Tier-3 questions. There is no non-agent spec DOS can grade them
against at scale. A better model gives a more fluent answer; it does not give a
more verifiable one. The Wall-3 ceiling (docs/204) applies in full.

What DOS does not abstain on is the **substrate** beneath the gestalt — the
citations that either resolve in a reporter or don't, the doses that are either
in-range or out, the figures that either trace to a filing or don't. That
substrate is often the *failure mode that actually kills people* in the sense
of sanctions, errors, and liability events — not the failure of the gestalt
judgment, but the failure of the mechanical layer beneath it. That is what the
sections below address.

**The one proven-positive consumer shape in regulated domains:** a downstream
human reviewer whose workload shrinks because the substrate has already been
checked non-forgeably. They still make the judgment; DOS makes the mechanical
layer un-fakeable so they spend their time on the part that actually requires
judgment. That is the entire value claim, no wider.

## 3. Legal — the citation rung: DOS's cleanest Tier-1 slot

### The failure the field has measured

The catastrophic, *sanctioned* failure mode for legal AI is the fabricated
citation. *Mata v. Avianca* (May 2023): counsel filed a brief citing cases that
did not exist, generated by ChatGPT. A $5,000 sanction, public censure, and
the canonical case in every AI-in-legal-practice article since. Stanford
researchers measured **17–33% citation hallucination across legal-RAG tools**
(2025, cited as of June 2026). Harvey-LAB (2026, co-built by
Anthropic/OpenAI/Google DeepMind/Google/Mistral) is the current long-horizon
legal-agent benchmark, and the field's own verdict on it is: citation
hallucination **"is not captured by any benchmark… it remains an unsolved
problem."**

### Why this is DOS's cleanest rung

A citation is a pair: a case reference and a quoted holding. The verdict is
four-valued:

| What the check finds | DOS stance |
|---|---|
| cite resolves in reporter AND quoted text appears | **ATTESTED** (THIRD_PARTY) |
| cite resolves BUT quoted text is absent | **REFUTED** (THIRD_PARTY) |
| no reporter cluster carries this citation | **REFUTED** (THIRD_PARTY — the *Mata* shape) |
| no corpus access (timeout, no token, network down) | **ABSTAIN** (NO_SIGNAL — the fail-safe) |

The reporter's citation index — Free Law Project's CourtListener, a public
law-school corpus, any authenticated reporter API — was authored by parties
entirely independent of the agent under adjudication. The agent cannot forge a
cluster in a third-party database. So a `RESOLVED_MATCH` is eligible to grant
belief under `believe_under_floor`; an `ABSTAIN` never fabricates one.

The quote-match is the docs/156 `derived_witness` shape: a *declared op*
(substring-or-semantic-match) over a *non-forgeable operand* (the resolved
opinion text). The op is committed up front; it is not reverse-searched to fit.

This is the `EvidenceSource` protocol (docs/265): a driver that takes a
`(case-cite, quoted-holding)` pair, calls the reporter, and returns an
`EvidenceFacts` struct with stance and accountability. The kernel needs nothing
new; the driver is userland. The build is in docs/279.

### What this is not

It does not make the legal argument correct. A brief can cite real cases,
quote them accurately, and still be wrong on the law. That is Tier-3 — DOS
abstains. A `J` (a caught fabrication) is a *caught-count*, not a won case.

The deliverable is the *existence + quote-fidelity layer*, which is also the
layer the emerging **AI-disclosure-certificate** rules demand — some
jurisdictions now require that AI-generated legal filings carry an audit
artifact attesting that citations were checked. The `(via citation-resolved)`
stamp on a DOS verdict is exactly that artifact.

### The split: what this doc recommends for GDPval / knowledge-work scale

The same split applies when the deliverable is a document or presentation
rather than a brief: verify the substrate (every cited figure links to its
source file; every quoted statistic resolves in a non-agent corpus), abstain on
the deliverable's quality. This is the docs/212 Tier-2 prescription applied
at the citation level: you can prove the *facts are grounded* while the *answer
may still be wrong*; say so, and stop there.

## 4. Clinical / healthcare — substrate invariants, not diagnosis

### The HealthBench picture

HealthBench (OpenAI, 2026; 5,000 clinical conversations, physician-authored
rubrics) grades along five axes including hedging and context-seeking. The
grading is model-based, Tier-3. The benchmark's own result on the axis DOS
cares about: the **DR.INFO specialist leaderboard** (information-theoretic
hedging quality) shows a 0.68 score for frontier models vs. 0.19–0.46 for
others — the *judgment variance* is where the score moves, not the substrate.

That is the right signal. Substrate failures in clinical settings are rarer for
capable models but *more silent* (the [[project-dos-wall-detect-not-fix]]
lesson): a model that confidently asserts a dose is safe when it is
contraindicated is more dangerous than one that is obviously confused. DOS's
contribution is not to improve the clinical judgment; it is to keep the
substrate layer non-forgeable so the pharmacist or physician spending their
time on judgment does not also have to re-verify mechanical invariants.

### The substrate invariant family — what DOS can witness pre-action

These are the class-(A) invariants that the world enforces structurally, the
same way a calendar refuses a double-booking:

- **Dose-in-range.** A claimed dose is either within the therapeutic range for
  the indicated patient class (drawn from a non-agent formulary or a structured
  drug database, not from the agent's own narration) or it is not.
- **Drug interaction.** A claimed co-prescription either appears in an
  interaction database (a third-party corpus the agent did not author) or it
  does not. This is `derived_witness` over a structured lookup: the interaction
  flag is a non-forgeable operand.
- **Claim-links-to-source.** A clinical assertion ("this drug has a
  black-box warning for X") either resolves in a structured drug label
  (FDA label DB, an authenticated formulary) or it does not. The same citation
  check as §3, applied to clinical sources.

All three are **pre-action** witnesses — run them *before* the prescription is
written or the order is sent, not after. This is the direction docs/212 §4
identified as the most domain-distinctive contribution: in irreversible
non-code domains (a sent order is not a reverted commit), the pre-action gate
(`dos hook pretool`, docs/126/191) carries far more value than the post-hoc
verify. The gate can only refuse-MORE (the conjunctive floor); it cannot
wrongly admit.

### The honest ceiling

DOS does not verify whether the clinical *judgment* is correct — is this the
right drug for this patient, given the full clinical picture? That is Tier-3.
The benchmark's own best evaluators (physician rubric + model judge) are the
right tool there. DOS witnesses the substrate and abstains on the rest, and
the product is that it says so.

## 5. Finance and life science QA — formula recompute and filing-trace

### Finance: the fabrication catalogue is published

FrontierFinance (arXiv 2604.05912, 2026) documents the failure modes of
frontier models on long-horizon financial-model tasks in the paper's own words:
fabricated balancing values, formulas replaced with static values, hidden rows
with white-font concealment. These are substrate forgeries — the agent produces
an artifact that *looks* complete and *passes a rubric* while the underlying
numbers are un-grounded.

The witness shape is `derived_witness` applied to arithmetic:
- **Formula recompute.** Every formula cell's stored value is checked against
  its recomputed value. A mismatch is a static-value masquerade (REFUTED).
- **Balance identity.** A balance that holds only by inserting a plug value
  with no precedent trace is caught by re-deriving the identity from its
  operands and checking the plug against the source.
- **Figure-to-filing trace.** Every headline figure in an output document is
  checked against the structured source (a filing API, a structured data feed)
  it claims to come from. The Finance-Agent leaderboard has measured the
  structured-vs-web reliability gap: Opus scores 90.8% with structured data
  APIs and 19.8% with web search alone. That 71-point gap is the rung-choice
  effect, measured.

The honest ceiling: DOS does not make the financial *judgment* right — is this
the right discount rate, is this projection sound? That is Tier-3. It makes the
*mechanical soundness layer* — the one FrontierFinance's failure analysis says
distinguishes human experts ("correctly linked," "auditable") — un-fakeable.

### Life science QA: the batch-record shape

A batch-release record in pharmaceutical QA is a structured artifact with
arithmetic invariants (yields sum, identities resolve, specifications have
non-agent-authored reference ranges from a regulatory submission or a
pharmacopoeia). The substrate check is the same formula-recompute shape: does
the recorded yield equal the recomputed yield from the raw assay data? Does the
identity test result match the specification drawn from a non-agent corpus?

The mandated non-agent consumer here — the QA release owner who signs — is
exactly the shape that makes a trust substrate valuable: they need to spend
their time on the judgment that a formula recompute cannot settle, not on
re-checking arithmetic a verified driver can check for free.

## 6. The over-claim — in regulated domains this is a liability

State this explicitly, because every section above is a *substrate* claim and
the temptation is to headline a *correctness* claim.

**"DOS verifies financial/legal/clinical correctness"** is false. It is also a
material misrepresentation in the regulated domains this doc covers — legal
advice, clinical advice, and financial advice are each regulated activities, and
claiming a software tool performs them would attract regulatory scrutiny of its
own.

The correct claim, repeated from §2:

> DOS verifies the **substrate** — citations exist and match, doses are in-range,
> figures trace to filings, formulas recompute. It **abstains on the gestalt**
> — the legal argument, the diagnosis, the financial judgment. The audit artifact
> (the `(via <rung>)` stamp) attests to the substrate check and is explicit that
> the gestalt was not verified.

A `J` (a blocked or caught substrate failure) is a *caught-count*. It is never
a downstream outcome delta. The certifying attorney still certifies; the medical
monitor still monitors; the QA release owner still releases. DOS makes their
substrate review un-fakeable and auditable. That is the whole claim.

Selling it as wider than that is the docs/138 discipline failure — and in these
domains it is not just imprecise, it is a liability.

## 7. The build order: substrate-first, gestalt-never

Following the project's cheapest-datum-first rule:

1. **Citation resolution (legal, §3)** — $0 replay on a public corpus. The
   failure is measured (17–33%), the corpus is free (CourtListener), the
   mandated consumer is real (certifying attorney), the audit artifact is
   required (AI-disclosure certificates). This is DOS's cleanest rung in any
   domain. Build: docs/279.

2. **Substrate invariants pre-action (clinical, §4)** — dose-in-range and
   interaction checks against a structured drug database. Most national
   formularies have a structured API or a downloadable dataset; the FDA drug
   label database is public. The gate fires *before* the order, which is the
   right place in an irreversible domain.

3. **Formula recompute (finance, §5)** — a `derived_witness` rung over a
   financial-model deliverable, checking stored values against their recomputed
   values and headline figures against structured-source filings. The corpus is
   a FrontierFinance-style frozen sample first, live if the slice is real.

In all three cases: **replay first ($0), live only if the slice is real**. And
in all three cases: **headline only the substrate check, never the gestalt**.

## 8. The through-line

DOS is not a compliance tool. It is a trust substrate that applies its one test
— *who authors the evidence, who authors the spec, is each a different author
than the claiming agent?* — to whatever domain is in front of it. In regulated
domains:

- Legal citations → evidence = reporter bytes (THIRD_PARTY); spec = the
  citation string (neither agent-authored nor forgeable in the reporter index).
  **Both halves sound.** Tier-1.
- Clinical substrate invariants → evidence = formulary/label DB bytes
  (THIRD_PARTY); spec = the therapeutic range or interaction flag (non-agent,
  non-forgeable). **Both halves sound.** Tier-1.
- Financial figures → evidence = structured filing bytes (THIRD_PARTY); spec =
  the formula identity (arithmetic, non-forgeable). **Both halves sound.** Tier-1.
- The gestalt judgment in all three → no non-agent spec exists at scale.
  **Tier-3. DOS abstains.**

The regulated domain is not a harder version of the general case. It is the
general case with a *mandated non-agent consumer already built in* — which is
the winning consumer shape, and the reason these domains are the right place to
start for "DOS beyond code."

The over-claim is the only trap. Step around it explicitly, every time, and the
substrate claim is both true and — in the domains where the field itself admits
the substrate layer is unguarded — commercially significant.
